# Anatomy

Adapted for ChatGPT/Codex from [Ryan's Anatomy](https://github.com/wheresryan22/anatomy). Preserve the bundled MIT notice. Preserve the bundled kit unchanged and use it where appropriate. **Do not force that engineering-oriented renderer onto molecular objects**: in BIOLOGICAL mode, choose suitable structural-geometry or molecular-surface rendering (including external 3D libraries when the output and environment permit), optionally alongside the kit for labels, views and controls. Preserve attribution for any copied kit code; do not silently update upstream dependencies. The source revision and adaptation boundaries are in [references/upstream.md](references/upstream.md).

Create a richly constructed physical subject whose structure and behavior explain the idea. **Select the representation before choosing how to draw it**: BIOLOGICAL for real proteins/cells/tissue and organic conformational mechanisms; MECHANISTIC for engineered systems or explicitly requested mechanical analogies; HYBRID for an aligned comparison when it genuinely improves learning. A causal mechanism is not necessarily a mechanical-looking object. The default target is the published Anatomy gallery's level of construction and motion, not a minimally functioning diagram. A quick first build is a prototype, not the finished deliverable. For an abstract concept, use a clearly identified analogy. For a real machine or biological specimen, preserve its actual relationships. The figure, caption, controls, and readout must agree.

## Priorities

Correctness, explanatory clarity, working interaction, and visual craft are independent acceptance gates. Keep factual truth when they conflict, then find a better construction or presentation rather than silently abandoning the visual target. **An accurate simplified diagram is not a substitute for an explicitly requested crafted, interactive, structural or isometric explanation.** A dense drawing with a misleading mechanism fails; a correct but unfinished collection of primitives also fails.

- Start from the subject as a three-dimensional physical assembly, not a slide or dashboard. Budget detail for the subject first and interface second. Preserve the conspicuous assembly relationships even when the output is small.
- Use coherent perspective, true occlusion, clear material relationships and soft contact shadows. Use the kit's four-tone solids for designed machinery; **do not force machined shapes or a grayscale palette on proteins**. Organic molecular subjects require differentiated chain/domain surfaces, credible interfaces and movement-dependent conformational changes when these are central to the explanation.
- Detail should explain material, assembly, scale, or function. Typical gallery part counts describe complexity; they are not quotas. A smooth real surface and useful negative space are valid; they do not excuse missing joinery, generic silhouettes, or unmodelled working internals. Match the complexity of the actual subject and user request.
- Prioritize structural exploration. Prefer assembled, sectional/cutaway, subsystem focus, and exploded/part-separated views when those reveal genuine interfaces. A scene without genuine depth/assembly exposure cannot be rescued by more explanatory cards. Every view must share the same subject and source of state.
- Keep one visual focus. Engineering materials may call for neutral/brass ramps; biological molecules often benefit from a rich but controlled, **persistent subunit-specific colour palette** that tracks identity through motion. Pair semantic colour with labels, patterns or shapes; never demand monochrome simply because an old benchmark used it.
- Labels normally sit outside the illustration. Use a small number of anchored callouts when identification is essential; accuracy and accessibility take precedence over the gallery's no-label convention.
- Dynamic means cause and effect are visible. More simultaneous motion, faster loops, camera drift, and extra particles do not inherently improve a figure.

## Build workflow

1. **Define the explanation and SELECT REPRESENTATION.** In brief task notes, state the audience, subject, central relationship, primary action, output format, and the visible consequence of that action. Choose BIOLOGICAL / MECHANISTIC / HYBRID using `references/representation-routing.md`, and record the rationale. A request to explain how a protein works defaults to a biological-looking protein; it does not imply a gearbox. Infer reasonable defaults; ask only for missing information that materially changes the work. Default to light for a new standalone page unless context specifies a theme.
2. **Establish truth.** Separate user parameters, sourced facts, derived values, and illustrative choices. Use a compact claim/source ledger for consequential facts. Specify units, valid ranges, model limits, and the invariants to test. Do not infer scientific correctness from an example, a plausible animation, or a passing geometry audit.
3. **Design the selected representation.** Choose references separately for the subject's *real appearance*, construction quality and interaction pattern. For molecular subjects, read `references/molecular-rendering.md` and favor source-based assemblies or explicitly schematic organic forms; examine the earlier ATP illustration for interaction/cutaway craft **without copying its mechanical geometry as the biological style ceiling**. A user-supplied Claude ATP video suggests useful molecular richness/colour/motion, but does not establish structural or kinetic accuracy. Use `references/atp-benchmark.md` for transferable controls; do not default to the shortest example just because its code is shortest. Write the primary form, working assemblies, their interfaces, and finish before paths. For an analogy, identify where the mapping breaks. For a specimen, establish compartments, continuity, orientation, and intentional cut surfaces. For animation, storyboard input → transmission/interface → visible change → stable result. Name the coupled moving parts, rather than settling for one floating marker. For an unfamiliar or dense composition, compare two rendered camera/cutaway blockouts and choose the one that reveals the critical relationships.
4. **Build a coherent first view.** Copy the appropriate implementation example and required resources to a task workspace, retaining relative paths and LICENSE. Adapt subject, geometry, captions, and accessibility text. Set one world coordinate system and camera; fit the subject and its motion envelope. World units are drawing units unless explicitly calibrated to physical units. Get one mechanism and its model working, then complete a representative junction and curved surface to the intended finish before propagating that construction style. Keep the subject large; add the surrounding interface after the picture can carry the explanation. Do not lay out a sidebar, extra teaching cards, or broad coloured panels until the central artifact looks convincing by itself at the intended display size.
5. **Use one source of state.** Keep meaningful state transitions in a pure model. Derive geometry, quantities, captions, and accessible descriptions from the same snapshot. Separate input targets from actual state and display easing. Add tests of domain invariants and boundary conditions before making the scene dense.
6. **Construct and shade for the mode.** Precompute static geometry. For MECHANISTIC use the kit's solids, lathes, tubes and material ramps. For BIOLOGICAL use credible chain/domain molecular volumes, surface/ribbon detail and scientifically meaningful subunit colours, with PDB-derived biological assemblies when available. For HYBRID use *one underlying state* in two aligned and distinctly labelled representations. Moving geometry uses world transforms or appropriate rebuilds, never a screen-space shortcut that breaks its perspective or attachment. Split wrapping shapes into back/front pieces; use recorded true shapes and applicable depth/clearance audits. Read engineering guidance before dense pipework, shaders, or 3D.
7. **Compose and animate the real causal changes.** Keep the chosen representation readable at normal size and on a phone. Proteins may rotate, bend, close/open binding pockets and alter interfaces in a coordinated sequence; do not substitute rigid rotations or random jitter for biologically meaningful conformational changes. Provide context-preserving assembly/subassembly views when the mechanism is dense, and genuine sectional, exploded, and focus views when tiny structures need explanation, with actual interior detail to inspect. A magnified coarse primitive is not a detail view. Give time-dependent sequences visible play/pause and replay controls; add steps or scrubbing when they help learning. Direct manipulation must work with pointer, keyboard, and touch. Give controls a visible relationship to the object; keep native accessible equivalents. Use multiple coordinated motions, slow inspection, and sectional or apart poses where the mechanism needs them. Follow the animation state and timing rules; do not substitute decorative continuous motion for a complete causal action.
8. **Review and repair.** First pass the **mode-fit gate** in `references/representation-routing.md`: an attractive, technically correct engineered illustration is not a successful molecular rendering if the task calls for an actual protein. Apply the composition, construction, behavior, and finish gates in `references/gallery-lessons.md` **and the independent visual-craft regression gate in `references/evaluation-rubric.md`**. If revising an existing artifact, preserve its strengths through side-by-side visual comparisons; do not count added explanation or more modern interface styling as a net improvement when the working mechanism gets smaller, flatter, or less explorable. Validate the model, geometry, layout, interaction, and motion separately. Inspect the overview, important close-ups, and transition boundaries. Compare the rendered subject against a suitable benchmark at a similar visual size, theme, and pose. For ATP synthase and closely analogous molecular mechanisms, compare with the archived earlier artifact for explorability, but use the new organic-rendering criteria for molecular fidelity; log retained, improved and regressed properties and distinguish purposeful style changes. Record and repair specific visible gaps; a passing test or polished toolbar is not this comparison. Fix the most consequential defect first: misleading structure, incorrect state, occlusion, unreadable content, discontinuity, then finish. Do not fill empty areas simply to increase detail.
9. **Deliver the requested artifact.** Save the working standalone file or project integration, with attribution and concise relevant model assumptions/sources. Include a still only when useful. Report which checks ran and any specific unresolved limitation. Do not claim model superiority or visual parity from syntax checks or an unviewed screenshot.

## Choose a rendering mode that meets the visual target

| Need | Mode | Starting point |
|---|---|---|
| Static technical figure | Standalone SVG with embedded styles | `examples/dial-indicator/build.mjs` |
| Translation, gauges, cutaways, sequences | SVG and a small JS controller in standalone HTML | `examples/dial-indicator/`, `examples/arcade-cabinet/` |
| Effects that need a continuous field | SVG with WebGL in the same world camera | `examples/raptor-engine/`, `examples/ripple-tank/` |
| True changes of orientation in depth | Optional beta 3D runtime for the necessary moving parts | `examples/turning-dial/` |
| Existing React/Next.js project | Static art plus one client controller | `examples/test-rig/`, `examples/spot-plate/` |
| Biological overview and a staged process | Subject-specific organic geometry, connected compartments, focus views | `references/biological-figures.md`; do not copy the legacy cell geometry |

Rendering economy is useful, but it must not reduce needed geometry or motion. `examples/desk-computer/` demonstrates face-aligned text and power transitions. Inspect the closest build/model/controller, not every example. Node 20.10+ is needed for bundled builds; Chrome/Chromium for browser checks. No model API key is required. Use only tools actually available in the target host.

For a constructed molecular motor exemplar, inspect `examples/atp-synthase/atp-synthase.html` and `references/atp-benchmark.md`. Its cutaway and part-exploration design set an *interaction and craft* target, not a molecular structure or kinetic truth source. The retained animal-cell example is a legacy schematic, not a gallery-quality template. Inspection found a tray-like composition, slab-shaped organelles, and a process overlay that cannot generally respect internal occlusion. Use its text and control structure only after checking them; rebuild subject geometry, compartment interfaces, and process depth for a new biological figure. Its `--check` covers a limited model contract, not biological topology. Do not present its eight-stage timing as kinetics.

## Acceptance before delivery

**Non-regression and adaptation rule:** for a revision, compare the old and new rendered working mechanism directly, excluding surrounding chrome. **Do not preserve a mechanical-looking biological figure as the universal design baseline when the task is to improve its organic fidelity.** If the new version loses meaningful assembly detail, cutaway depth, useful focus modes, correct occlusion, or a previously functional inspection workflow, treat this as a blocking regression unless the user requested the simplification. Scientific/technical corrections should be made *within* a comparably capable visual experience, not paid for by flattening the model.


- **Representation fitness:** the chosen BIOLOGICAL, MECHANISTIC or HYBRID form suits the referent, the request and evidence; biologically real proteins are not reduced to machinery by default. Record data provenance separately from artistic fidelity.
- **Accuracy:** consequential claims are traceable; assumptions and visual simplifications are explicit; quantities have defensible units and precision; invariants and limiting cases agree with the lesson.
- **Geometry:** actual rendered shapes are represented in applicable audits. Report sampled poses and tolerances, not a proof of every possible continuous motion. No false surrogate shapes or loosened checks to obtain a pass.
- **Polish:** clear silhouette and hierarchy at intended size, coherent curvature and materials, readable text, stable readout layout, no clipped controls, and useful inspection at 390 CSS px. Inspect both themes if both are shipped.
- **Animation:** one causal timeline/state model; no unexplained teleport, cargo disappearance, attachment drift, or discontinuity at phase boundaries. Test pause/resume, restart, rapid reversal, and endpoints. Where phase velocity should be continuous, test it as well as position.
- **Accessibility:** native labelled controls where possible, visible focus, keyboard and touch operation, stable reduced-motion steps, and no autoplay restarting after an explicit pause. Announce meaningful changes, not every animation frame.
- **Performance/fallback:** stop idle and hidden work, avoid duplicate loops, handle resize and teardown, and retain an intelligible SVG fallback when WebGL is unavailable. Measure performance on the actual test setup before claiming a rate.
- **Evidence:** distinguish model, geometry, static visual, and real-browser results. A successful build or test prompt is not an executed visual evaluation. If the browser is unavailable, deliver useful work with its precise verification limits.
