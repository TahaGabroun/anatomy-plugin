## What the adaptation was missing

It already contained the upstream kit. The main gap was how reliably it directed the model to use it:

- “Smallest adequate mode” and starting from the smallest example could be mistaken for a low-detail target. Rendering economy and visual ambition are different decisions.
- Correctness and accessibility requirements were actionable; visual quality was mostly adjectives. A working control and a clean build could become an accidental stopping point.
- The parts list did not force a hierarchy of enclosure, working assembly, interfaces and finish. This encourages isolated recognisable icons on a slab.
- Animation policy explained clocks and pause well, but not how to compose a convincing action, transmit forces or cargo, and expose the result.
- A copied example could dominate the design even when its geometry was unsuitable. The added cell example exemplifies this risk: its cut plane and low extrusions create a tray-like arrangement, while its process is drawn above all static art in one overlay.

## Concrete authoring method

Use this during design, implementation, and visual review. Keep the notes short; spend effort on geometry and renders.

### 1. Choose the camera by what it reveals

List the two or three relationships that must be seen together. Block out their large volumes in the real coordinate system. For a dense or unfamiliar subject, render two plausible camera/cutaway arrangements before committing. Prefer the one with fewer overlaps hiding the lesson. Do not generate gratuitous variants for a straightforward composition.

Preserve an enclosing silhouette and show the selected interior through an intentional opening. A removed panel should have plausible thickness, a cut rim, and a relationship to its host. Avoid transparent x-ray clutter as the default. Reserve room for the motion envelope and one useful inspection state.

### 2. Establish four levels of construction

For each relevant assembly, identify:

1. **Primary form:** the recognisable enclosure or specimen volume.
2. **Working structure:** the pieces that perform the explanation.
3. **Interfaces:** bearings, seats, pivots, mounts, ports, boundaries, or tissue continuities that connect those pieces.
4. **Finish:** meaningful seams, bevels, fasteners, knurls, scales, folds and surface features.

Not every object needs every kind of detail. But omitting interfaces while adding ornamental dots is a failure. Build one representative junction to final quality early, then reuse its geometry and material conventions. This prevents dozens of inconsistently improvised joints.

For literal biology, these are enclosing surfaces, connected compartments, interfaces, and characteristic internal structure. Metal slabs with rounded corners do not become cisternae by renaming them. If the primitive vocabulary cannot express the structure, write suitable geometry instead of forcing the subject into the vocabulary.

### 3. Make the mechanism legible without narration

Temporarily judge the scene without the explanatory paragraph. Can a reader locate the input, see what changes, and distinguish the outcome? Use object geometry and spatial state first, then a short hint and readout. A select menu is a useful alternate control, not the visual explanation.

Keep controls compact and subordinate. Do not use a large sidebar or a wall of text to compensate for a tiny subject. On a phone, choose a deliberate focus/detail view where an overview cannot explain the critical interface. Magnifying coarse geometry only exposes its coarseness; improve the detail that the close view is meant to teach.

### 4. Choreograph an action with a consequence

Write a short chain: input → transmission/interface → visible change → stable result. The chosen analogy must support it. Motion may continue where the model calls for it, but the central result must be inspectable.

- Use several coordinated motions when one mechanism requires them; “one primary action” does not mean “one animated part.”
- In a machine, constrained gears, shafts, springs and indicators move from shared state with meaningful phase relationships.
- In a process, preserve object identity while it changes shape, enclosure or material state. A marker moving over intact geometry is not a replacement for crossing a real interface.
- Use slow mode, a sectional view, or an exploded inspection pose when it reveals an otherwise unreadable action. Do not add 3D solely to advertise complexity.
- Keep accents/effects local: the relevant hot body, lamp, water volume, or active interface. The effect must register with surfaces and obey occlusion.

Read `dynamic-animation.md` for timing, lifecycle and event handling. Test the action itself as well as its buttons.

### 5. Finish the form before decorating the page

At normal output size, establish large light/shade masses, readable silhouettes, and one focal assembly. Then inspect curved transitions and joinery at 4×. Use the kit's close ramps with sufficient silhouette contrast; fading everything equally makes it look unfinished. Avoid thick black edges and arbitrary gloss as a shortcut.

Use monochrome when it serves the object. Add controlled material colour when it distinguishes brass, glass, rubber, fluid or the teaching subject; keep values, light direction, and shading consistent. A colour-coded interface still needs a non-colour cue. User branding takes precedence over the gallery palette when requested.

## Completion gates

When reworking an existing illustration, use the earlier rendered artifact as a baseline. If the revision simplifies the physical model or eliminates investigative views while adding information cards, the **construction and exploration gates fail**, even if model-validation scripts pass.


These are quality gates, not user permission gates. Iterate within the authorized task.

- **Composition:** the object and working relationship are readable before microdetail. Repair a wrong camera or silhouette before adding text.
- **Construction:** exposed parts are connected and have plausible thickness and interfaces. Check an overview and representative junction close-ups.
- **Behavior:** one complete action demonstrates cause and consequence; direct manipulation, inspection, and replay preserve state and attachments.
- **Finish:** compare a render to a suitable reference at similar subject size, theme and pose. Identify specific gaps in depth, interface detail, material treatment and movement, then fix the most consequential gaps and re-render.

Do not require a quota of repair iterations when the output already meets the target. Do not declare gallery-level quality while a known major visual defect remains. If tooling prevents observation, identify that limitation rather than treating code assertions as visual acceptance. A benchmark is a craft target, not a guarantee of parity with its author or model.
